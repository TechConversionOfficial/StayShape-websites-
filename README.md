## The problem

A fitness website needs to do more than look energetic. People considering online coaching want to know **who will train them, which format fits their routine, what help they receive, how sessions work and how to begin**.

For someone new to exercise, a generic photograph of a gym does not explain whether training is suitable for a beginner, can be done at home, or includes individual guidance.

The website therefore needs a recognisable trainer-led proposition, a clear breakdown of training formats, relevant coaching paths and a low-friction way to ask about joining.

The supplied website captures support a visual comparison between an earlier and redesigned presentation. They do not independently prove that the earlier version caused lower engagement or that the redesign increased sign-ups or conversions.

## How Tech Conversion presented the redesigned experience

The points below describe what is visible in the supplied StayShape project media together with the agency's public project description. They are not evidence that every interaction is deployed, working or converting at a particular rate.

1. **Lead with a human, trainer-centred first screen.** The redesign replaces generic gym imagery with a fitness-coach-led hero and the direct message **“Stay in Shape”**.
2. **Make the online training proposition explicit.** The design introduces StayShape as an online fitness brand rather than assuming visitors already know whether it is a physical gym, recorded video library or guided coaching service.
3. **Keep one clear primary action.** A prominent *Book Free 3 Day Trial* action appears in the project presentation. This should be reconciled with the current offer before reuse in advertisements or live copy.
4. **Provide immediate service reassurance.** Modules highlight **Personalised Training Plans**, **Home & Online Workouts**, **Expert Guidance** and **Real Results**. Claims of expertise and results require evidence and should not become guaranteed health outcomes.
5. **Allow visitors to explore their goals.** Goal-based cards illustrate paths around weight loss, strength, core stability and staying active. Any live programme segmentation should reflect actual services without making assumptions about individual ability or gender.
6. **Balance motivational visuals with readable information.** The redesign uses higher-contrast headings, supporting white space, bright action accents and clearer content hierarchy.
7. **Consider smaller-screen visitors.** Phone and tablet presentations indicate responsive design intent, but do not prove independent device testing or WCAG compliance.
8. **Support a subscription or enquiry journey.** The presentation supports programme discovery and enquiry, while a functioning member dashboard, subscription checkout, automated billing, booking calendar or onboarding system remain unverified.

## Quality and safety standards to evaluate

These are **recommended website-review criteria**, not statements that testing, accessibility certification or health qualification checks were completed.

- **[Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals):** assess training images, video embeds, hero loading, mobile layout stability and first interaction time.
- **[WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/):** review accessible navigation, form labels, colour contrast, video captions, meaningful image descriptions and focus indicators.
- **[Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials):** evaluate clear training-service descriptions, appropriate programme landing pages and discoverable contact information.
- **[Schema.org](https://schema.org/):** consider accurate Organisation, Person, Service, BreadcrumbList and other relevant markup where supported by real content.
- **Qualifications and programme scope:** verify professional credentials, advertised session types, location/availability and external memberships before publication.
- **Health and fitness claims:** avoid guaranteed weight loss, spot-reduction, injury-prevention or medical-treatment claims. Programmes should encourage suitable assessment and qualified advice where appropriate.
- **Training and nutrition privacy:** if users provide body measurements, health information, dietary needs or progress photos, review secure data collection, consent and applicable privacy rules.
- **Subscription and trial transparency:** before accepting payment, present current prices, session inclusions, renewal terms, cancellation/refund rules and exact trial eligibility. A trial banner does not demonstrate a deployed subscription platform.
