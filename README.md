## The problem

A fitness website needs to do more than look energetic. People considering online coaching want to know **who will train them, which format fits their routine, what help they receive, how sessions work and how to begin**.

For someone new to exercise, a generic photograph of a gym does not explain whether training is suitable for a beginner, can be done at home, or includes individual guidance.

A site that combines dark imagery, long blocks of motivational copy and indistinct calls to action can make the first decision harder. The website needs a more recognisable trainer-led proposition, a simple breakdown of training formats, relevant coaching paths and a low-friction way to ask about joining.

This challenge is supported by the saved StayShape comparison artwork and Tech Conversion's portfolio positioning. ## How Tech Conversion presented the redesigned experience

The points below describe **what is visible in saved StayShape project imagery** together with the agency's public project description. They are not evidence that every interaction is deployed, working or converting at a particular rate.

1. **Lead with a human, trainer-centred first screen.** The *after* concept replaces a generic gym image with a fitness-coach figure and the direct message **“Stay in Shape”**. The focus moves towards a service delivered by people, not just exercise imagery.

2. **Make the online training proposition explicit.** The design introduces StayShape as an online fitness brand rather than assuming that visitors already know whether it is a physical gym, recorded video library or guided coaching service.

3. **Keep one clear primary action.** A bright *Book Free 3 Day Trial* action appears above the fold. This is a design proposal and must be reconciled with the current offer before reuse in advertisements or live copy.

4. **Provide immediate service reassurance.** Compact modules highlight **Personalised Training Plans**, **Home & Online Workouts**, **Expert Guidance** and **Real Results**. Claims of expertise and results require client evidence, and “real results” must not become a guaranteed health outcome.

5. **Allow visitors to explore their goals.** The tablet mockup uses **For Her** and **For Him** cards and examples such as weight loss, strength, core stability and staying active. Any deployed programme segmentation should reflect real training services without making assumptions about individual ability or gender.

6. **Balance motivational visuals with readable information.** The concept uses high-contrast headings, ample white space around supporting modules, bright teal action accents and a restrained colour scheme rather than relying entirely on black and orange.

7. **Consider smaller-screen visitors.** Phone and tablet views are visible in the same design presentation, indicating mobile layout planning. This should not be interpreted as proof of independent device testing or WCAG compliance.

8. **Support a subscription or enquiry journey.** Tech Conversion describes StayShape as a platform for people to discover and join training and discusses subscriber trust in its portfolio. The artwork does not verify a functioning member dashboard, subscription checkout, automated billing, calendar booking or onboarding system.

## Quality and safety standards to evaluate

These are **recommended website-review criteria**, not statements that testing, accessibility certification or health qualification checks were completed.

- **[Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals):** assess training images, video embeds, hero loading, mobile layout stability and first interaction time.
- **[WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/):** review accessible navigation, form labels, colour contrast, video captions, meaningful image descriptions and focus indicators.
- **[Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials):** evaluate clear training-service descriptions, appropriate programme landing pages and discoverable contact information.
- **[Schema.org](https://schema.org/):** consider accurate Organisation, Person, Service, BreadcrumbList and other relevant markup where supported by the real content.
- **Qualifications and programme scope:** verify professional credentials, advertised session types, location/availability and any external memberships before publication.
- **Health and fitness claims:** avoid guaranteed weight loss, spot-reduction, injury-prevention or medical-treatment claims. Ensure programmes encourage suitable assessment and qualified advice where appropriate.
- **Training and nutrition privacy:** if users provide body measurements, health information, dietary needs or progress photos, review secure data collection, consent and applicable privacy rules.
- **Subscription and trial transparency:** before accepting payment, present current prices, session inclusions, renewal terms, cancellation/refund rules and the exact trial eligibility. The design-stage trial banner does not demonstrate a deployed subscription platform.
